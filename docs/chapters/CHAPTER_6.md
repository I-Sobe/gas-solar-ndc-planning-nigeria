# Chapter 6 — Conclusions

## Summary of findings

This thesis constructed a deterministic linear-programming capacity-expansion model of Nigeria’s power sector to 2054, in which the division of each vintage’s investment capital between concessional and commercial sources is determined within the model rather than supplied to it, and used it to examine the four questions of §1.4. Four findings follow, each stated as the thesis establishes it and each resting on the results reported in Chapter 4 and interpreted in Chapter 5.

1.  The pathway is determined by the gas system, under a policy framed around renewable deployment. Of the fourteen ranked parameter families, nine are properties of the gas system — the condition of the existing fleet, the operations permitted on it, its retirement schedule, the quantity of fuel reaching it and the efficiency of conversion — and no solar-side parameter enters the ranked table at any position, with no policy in force or under a binding emissions commitment (§4.2.3, §4.2.6, §5.2.1). This does not make solar incidental: solar is the largest single line in the capital programme and the capacity built by 2045 is substantially solar. It establishes that what the plan builds and what determines what the plan costs fall on opposite sides of the fuel supply, and that uncertainty about the gas system propagates into cost at a scale no other family approach.

2.  The allocation of concessional capital carries most of a finite facility’s value, and the value of the private-capital channel is conditional on a public budget binding. Determining the division within the model, rather than supplying it, is worth between ⟦0.58⟧ and ⟦3.46⟧ per cent of system cost, rising with the size of the facility; at the facility’s actual size that is ⟦61⟧ per cent of what the facility is worth in total (§4.3.2, §5.3.1). The value lies in where concessional capital is placed rather than in how much of it there is, and the measurement is taken where no public capital budget binds, so the result does not depend on public capital being scarce.

> The service-financed channel behaves differently, and its conditionality is the principal result of the financing analysis (§1.5.3). The evidence is a separation rather than a point estimate: across ⟦36⟧ configurations, in the ⟦18⟧ cells where the public capital budget’s shadow price is zero the channel’s value is at most ⟦−0.61⟧ United States dollars — the agreement between two solutions of the same plan — and in the ⟦18⟧ cells where that shadow price is positive it never falls below ⟦653,600,175⟧ dollars. One constraint binding or not binding separates the two outcomes exactly. Where it binds, both financing regimes reach an identical optimum and the service-financed regime reaches it on ⟦60.0⟧ to ⟦62.8⟧ per cent less public capital.

3.  Emissions fall outside a grid-bounded commitment under two distinct determinants, which act under different commitments. Under the conditional arm of the current commitment the cap displaces demand on its own account: with no capital budget in force the central arm places ⟦12.76⟧ per cent of emissions outside the boundary, and adding a tight public capital budget changes that figure to ⟦12.65⟧ per cent. Under the unconditional arm the position reverses — ⟦0.14⟧ per cent without a budget, rising to ⟦3.91⟧ per cent with one (§4.4.2, §5.4.1). Where a commitment is stringent enough to bind on the capacity the system can physically build, the financing constraint adds little the cap has not already done; where it is not, the financing constraint is what relocates the emissions.

4.  A deterministically optimal plan carries no adequacy margin. A plan optimised against the central demand realisation and held fixed is infeasible against ⟦50⟧ of ⟦100⟧ sampled realisations, while the same draws solved with the plan free are feasible in all ⟦100⟧. The feasible and infeasible draws do not overlap in demand growth: every draw below the central assumption is feasible and every draw above it is not, with the assumption itself the boundary (§4.5.2, §5.5.1). The result follows from cost-minimisation under a single assumed future and is a property of the planning method rather than of this model’s parameter values. A planning process adopting such a plan has implicitly assumed that demand will not exceed its own central projection.

## 6.2 Contribution to knowledge, and its scope

The contribution is a capacity-expansion formulation in which the division of each vintage’s capital between a concessional and a commercial tranche is a decision variable, bounded by a finite concessional envelope and disciplined by a coverage-ratio condition on privately financed capacity. Each tranche is annualised at its own rate over its own tenor, and both capital recovery factors and their discount weights are precomputed constants, so the financing decision is a volume split at fixed prices and the programme remains linear (§1.5.1, §3.4.2).

Its significance rests on the corpus of optimisation-based power-system planning studies for sub-Saharan Africa identified by the systematic review of Chapter 2. In ⟦sixty-five⟧ of ⟦seventy-five⟧ studies the cost of capital is supplied as an input, ⟦63.1⟧ per cent of those supplying a value give no source or argument for it, and no study determines financing structure or the realised cost of capital within its model (§2.5.1, §2.5.2, §2.7.1). That omission matters because the quantity it fixes is not neutral: capital for energy infrastructure in developing economies differs by country, technology and delivery mode by enough to reorder a least-cost ranking, and a uniform cost-of-capital assumption biases the results of energy-system models (Egli et al., 2019; Steffen, 2020). A model carrying the cost of capital as a scalar cannot pose the allocation question at all, and §6.1 establishes that the question carries the majority of a finite facility’s value. The omission is therefore structural rather than a failure of documentation.

Three bounds attach to the claim and are stated here as §1.5.2 states them. No claim is made concerning the wider capacity-expansion literature, which the review did not search; the review’s own limitations — a retrieval failure of approximately one quarter of reports sought at full text, a pre-committed screening stop, and single-reviewer screening with two registered validation steps not executed — bound every statement of absence above (§2.6, §5.6). The restriction of concessional eligibility to renewable capital is a declared modelling assumption and not a rule of the facility from which the envelope’s scale is taken, with the consequence that the envelope is a renewable-capital instrument by construction and the model cannot identify a concessional dollar better deployed on gas capacity (§3.4.3). And the mechanism the service-financed channel represents is generic private capital mobilisation — budget decoupling together with a revenue-adequacy test — with nothing in the formulation specific to a service contract as distinct from any other structure moving capital off the public balance sheet (§3.4.6).

## 6.3 Priorities for further work

Section 5.9 sets out the full programme, ordered by what each item would resolve against what it would cost. Three stand above the rest.

A fuel-agnostic availability factor for the existing gas fleet, published at a resolution separating mechanical and atmospheric availability from feedstock unavailability, is the highest-value item in the thesis. It would narrow the model’s dominant prior, discharge the double duty the gas capacity credit currently performs, and permit the adequacy results to be read without the optimism condition of §3.7.4. The records are held by the system operator and the regulator, so the work is disclosure and classification rather than new measurement (§5.2.3, §5.7, §5.9).

A planning reserve margin sourced as such would replace an operating quantity presently used as a planning standard and fixed in megawatts against a growing peak, placing the adequacy results on a standard rather than on an analyst construction (§3.7.4, §5.5.4).

Solving the two-stage recourse problem, rather than bounding it from two evaluations, would separate the value of the stochastic solution from the value of perfect information and convert a bound into a decision input for a planner choosing how much to commit in advance (§5.5.3, §5.9).

## 6.4 Concluding remarks

The thesis set out to examine what limits the expansion of Nigeria’s grid, what determines the cost of that expansion, and what a commitment drawn at the grid boundary achieves. Its answer is not the one the policy framing anticipates. The commitment is expressed in renewable deployment and the plan the model builds is substantially solar, but the cost of that plan is set almost entirely on the gas side of the system — by whether the existing fleet can run, by how much fuel reaches it, and by what is done with that fuel once it arrives. A decarbonisation programme for this system is, in its cost structure, a gas programme.

The same asymmetry conditions the commitment. The emissions trajectory constructed here is anchored to a baseline that gas deliverability rather than demand determines, and the model meets that trajectory substantially because the system remains supply-constrained (§3.2.6, §3.5.2, §5.2). Compliance obtained in that way is not abatement, and a commitment framed at the grid boundary records it as though it were.
