# Phase 2.6 — genset backstop parameter memo

**Status: parameters derived, one blocker open. Do not write model code until §4
is closed.**

## 1. The finding that governs everything else

**ZE-Gen's fuel-consumption figures are not measurements. They are one assumed
parameter applied across every segment.**

Dividing each segment's reported monthly litres by its reported monthly kWh:

| Segment | Litres/month | kWh/month | Implied L/kWh | Stated fuel |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Household | 14.8 | 7.40 | **2.000** | mostly petrol |
| MSME | 15.0 | 7.50 | **2.000** | mostly petrol |
| School | 8.6 | 4.30 | **2.000** | mostly diesel |
| Health centre | 13.8 | 6.90 | **2.000** | mostly diesel |
| Hotel | 87.4 | 43.70 | **2.000** | mostly diesel |

Five segments, two different fuels with different densities and different
calorific values, and every one returns exactly 2.000. That is arithmetically
impossible for independently measured data. Page 5 states it plainly: *"a
standard load factor of 20% was applied across all clusters due to minimal
variation"* and *"approximately 2 litres of fuel required to generate 1 kWh."*

**Consequences:**

1. The **metered kWh is real and usable** — 500 smart meters, 12 months, five use
   cases. This is the best utilisation data available for Nigeria.
2. The **litres are derived**, and therefore so are the monthly fuel spends
   ($12.1 household, $12.2 MSME, $71.3 hotel) and the $8bn national fuel figure
   that is reconstructed from them. None of these is independent evidence for the
   SFC.
3. The assumed SFC implies **η_HHV = 5.1% on petrol and 4.7% on diesel.** For the
   small oversized part-loaded petrol sets that dominate the Nigerian household
   fleet this is high but not impossible. For a hotel's diesel set running 3.2
   hours daily it is not credible.

This is the same class of defect as the 23.08 TWh demand base and the 0.43 gas
efficiency: **a number generated by an assumption, not by a measurement, and
propagated because it was internally consistent.** It belongs in the discussion
chapter alongside those.

**ZE-Gen contradicts itself on this.** Page 3 states generator electricity is
"6× more expensive than the grid" (sourced to IFC 2019) and page 7 that fuel
costs are "roughly double the average cost of grid electricity." At 2 L/kWh and
NGN 1,200/l, fuel alone is NGN 2,400/kWh against a Band A tariff near NGN 209 —
approximately 11×, not 2×. The qualitative claims and the quantitative parameter
are inconsistent by a factor of five to six.

## 2. Emission factors — derived, and validated

Following the structure adopted in correction plan 1.2, `EF_e` is never stored.
`EF_th` on an HHV basis and `η_genset` are written to file; `EF_e = EF_th / η` is
derived at model build time. Specific fuel consumption is likewise derived as
`SFC = 1 / (HHV_per_litre × η)` and never sourced independently — sourcing both
would allow cost and emissions to drift apart under a sweep, which is precisely
the defect 1.2 fixed for the gas fleet.

| | PMS (motor gasoline) | AGO (gas/diesel oil) | Gas fleet (existing) |
|---|---|---|---|
| NCV (IPCC V2 Ch1 Table 1.2) | 44.3 TJ/Gg | 43.0 TJ/Gg | — |
| Density (NMDPRA, 15 °C) | 0.755 kg/l | 0.840 kg/l | — |
| NCV:GCV ratio (IPCC V2 Ch1 §1.4.1.2) | 0.95 | 0.95 | 0.9025 |
| **HHV per litre** | **9.780 kWh_th/l** | **10.561 kWh_th/l** | — |
| CO₂ EF, NCV basis (IPCC V2 Ch2) | 69,300 kg/TJ | 74,100 kg/TJ | 56,100 kg/TJ |
| **EF_th, HHV basis** | **0.23701 tCO₂/MWh_th** | **0.25342 tCO₂/MWh_th** | 0.1823 |

**Validation.** The chain reproduces **2.318 kgCO₂/litre for petrol** and
**2.676 kgCO₂/litre for diesel** against published values of approximately 2.31
and 2.68. The route through Table 1.2, the NMDPRA densities and the Table 2.x
factors is correct.

**Two notes on the IPCC tables.** The CO₂ default is identical across Tables 2.2
(Energy Industries), 2.3 (Manufacturing), 2.4 (Commercial/Institutional) and 2.5
(Residential) — only CH₄ and N₂O differ. Since CO₂ only is carried, for
comparability with the gas-fleet emissions cap, the source-category choice is
immaterial and this should be stated explicitly. Second, the liquid NCV:GCV
ratio is **0.95**, not the 0.9025 used for natural gas — IPCC §1.4.1.2 gives
approximately 5% for coal and oil against 9–10% for gas. Reusing the gas ratio
would have introduced a 5% error.

Each arm is a real Nigerian population, not an assumed ±X% band around a central
value. Full derivation in `data/cost/raw/genset_lcoe_buildup.xlsx`.

**`genset_high` is fully sourced and is the arm you should not use as central.**
At $3,323/MWh it sits at essentially the same level as the VoLL-dominated Monte
Carlo LCOE (~$3,600/MWh) already on the do-not-report list. A backstop priced
there does not convert the VoLL cliff into a slope; it renames it. The entire
sequencing argument for 2.6 preceding Phase 3 fails if `genset_high` is central.

**Nigerian capex, August 2026** (your quotes, FX 1,343, pf 0.8):

| Rating | NGN | USD | USD/kW |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 kVA | 300,000 | 223 | 140 |
| 3 kVA | 500,000 | 372 | 155 |
| 5 kVA | 800,000 | 596 | 149 |

These are far below the ~$600/kW that circulates in the modelling literature.
Locally observed Nigerian pricing is the stronger source for a Nigerian study and
should be used in preference. Note that ZE-Gen's stated expenditure split
(fuel 67% / capex 16% / O&M 17%) implies a ~$150 unit — 2.5× below your August
2026 quotes — so the published split understates capital and is not used.

## 4. The one open blocker

**`η_genset` for the two diesel arms is provisional and no cost result from
`genset_srmc` or `genset_central` is reportable until it is sourced.**

What closes it: **one manufacturer fuel-consumption curve** — litres per hour at
25%, 50%, 75% and 100% of rated load — for a representative C&I diesel set in the
size band that actually serves Nigerian commercial load. Firman is the dominant
brand in the ZE-Gen survey (33% of households, 88% of health centres); Mikano
assembles in Nigeria and publishes by kVA. That single document resolves η, and η
then determines SFC, fuel cost and EF_e together through the identity above.

Second, smaller: **capex, life, capacity factor and fixed O&M for the diesel
arms** are placeholders. The capacity factor matters twice — it divides the
capital recovery term and, through the operating-hours rating, sets the economic
life. It is the most consequential single input in the build-up.

## 5. Acceptance tests

**Test 1 — aggregate reconciliation. `genset_high` fails as a national
explanation, and that failure is informative.** The $8bn national SGG fuel spend
at $0.7311/l implies 10.94 bn litres/year; at 2.000 l/kWh that is **5.47 TWh/yr**
of self-generated electricity. NIRP's λ of 0.51–0.57 implies self-generation of
approximately **31.6 TWh/yr**. Small petrol gensets can account for at most a
sixth of Nigeria's self-generation by energy.

**The structural reading: self-generation is bimodal, and the two modes behave
completely differently.** Small petrol sets deliver very little energy at
catastrophic unit cost. Large C&I and captive diesel plant delivers most of the
energy at roughly a fifth of that cost. For a grid capacity-expansion model, the
marginal backstop for bulk unserved demand is the second mode — which is why
`genset_central` is the diesel arm. The petrol arm is the right price only for
the Tier 3 access dimension.

**Test 2 — behavioural. Passed.** Band A tariff NGN 209/kWh at FX 1,343 is
$155.6/MWh. `genset_central` is 3.75× that; `genset_high` is 21.4×. Both exceed
the tariff, as they must. The 21× ratio is a further signal that `genset_high`
describes a small, desperate energy volume rather than a bulk supply alternative.

**Test 3 — internal cost structure. Passed, with a caveat.** `genset_central` is
82% fuel, as expected for a higher-utilisation asset. `genset_high` is 44% fuel
and 56% capital-plus-O&M, as expected at a 0.42% capacity factor. The ordering is
correct. The caveat is that `genset_high`'s capacity factor is itself derived
from the metered 88.8 kWh/yr, which is sound, applied to a 2.4 kW rating, which
is a survey modal value rather than a metered one.

## 6. Design decisions carried into the code

6. **Units at the balance — DECIDED: coefficient 1.0.** The balance is metered
   at busbar; a genset delivers at the point of use, downstream of the 7% TLF,
   so 1 TWh of genset output displaces roughly 1.075 TWh of busbar generation.
   The model credits only 1.0.

   This is a declared conservative simplification, not an oversight, and the
   direction is stated deliberately: at 1.0 the backstop is less effective per
   TWh, so the model leans harder on grid supply, builds more solar, and
   reports a higher system cost. It therefore OVERSTATES the grid build the
   NDC requires — conservative with respect to the thesis's own feasibility
   claim rather than flattering to it.

   Sensitivity arm: `selfgen_busbar_factor = 1/(1-0.07) = 1.0752688`, run
   separately. Verified to wire correctly via
   `scripts/probe_2_6_structure.py --busbar 1.0752688172043012`.
