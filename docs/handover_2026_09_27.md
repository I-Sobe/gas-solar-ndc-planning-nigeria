## 1. What this project is, and what the current effort is for

A capacity-expansion model of Nigeria's power sector to 2054, with an
endogenous financing layer. MSc thesis, to be defended in a **petroleum
and energy resource engineering** department and submitted to a **Q1
journal**. Zeus's flagship output.

**Describe the architecture in full, not just its core.** Each solve is
a deterministic, single-objective LP — fixed coefficients, one objective
(minimise discounted system cost), perfect foresight. That core is
wrapped in three layers the thesis must claim, because they are what the
analysis actually consists of:

- **Bi-objective trade-offs by the ε-constraint method** on two
  objective pairs — cost–emissions (`18_`, 25 points × 4 financing arms)
  and cost–capital (`28_`, 19 points × 2 regimes). This *is* the
  standard multi-objective method: the frontier is the multi-objective
  result, assembled from single-objective solves, with duals, binding
  checks and non-dominance verification. A weighted-sum objective
  existed, was dead code, and was deleted — correctly, since
  scalarisation cannot reach non-convex regions of a frontier.
- **Three uncertainty treatments**: scenario uncertainty through the arm
  system and the 39-invocation sweep; parameter uncertainty through
  Sobol variance decomposition over 1,792 deterministic solves (`29_`);
  and **two-stage fixed-plan re-dispatch** (`30_`), which freezes the
  here-and-now investment decision and re-dispatches under Monte Carlo
  realisations, yielding VaR, CVaR and infeasibility-under-a-fixed-plan.
- **What is not claimed**: stochastic programming with recourse. The
  optimiser never hedges against a distribution inside a single LP. This
  is two-stage *evaluation*, not two-stage optimisation, and
  `src/stochastic.py`'s own docstring states the limitation.

So: "deterministic LP" is correct for the solver and wrong as a summary
of the method. Chapter 3 should say *a deterministic LP capacity-expansion
model, analysed under bi-objective ε-constraint trade-offs and three
distinct uncertainty treatments* — and §3.6 sets each out.

**The current effort is Chapter 3 — the methodology chapter.** Target
structure: Ch.1 Introduction, Ch.2 Literature review, **Ch.3
Methodology**, Ch.4 Results, Ch.5 Discussion, Ch.6 Conclusions, plus
appendices carrying the parameter provenance table, the structural
assumptions table, and the limitations register.

An earlier attempt drafted Chapter 3 from the project's own methodology
documents. That was wrong: those documents record what the project
*said* it built, not what it *has*. Two capabilities were missed
entirely and one was described from a stale copy after being deleted.
A nine-part **model capability inventory** was therefore run against the
source (`docs/model_capability_inventory.md`, 2,862 lines), and the
chapter is now written from that.

## 2. Standing principles — these govern everything

1. **Rigour over expedience.** Do not propose shortcuts. Do not suggest
   reporting stale results with a caveat when the alternative is fixing
   the scripts and re-running. Fix, extend and re-run rather than
   qualify — unless Zeus explicitly says otherwise for a specific case.
2. **Runs that produce citable results go in `scripts/*.py` runners**
   with the standard arm interface via `src/cli_arms.py` — not ad-hoc
   bash heredocs. Heredocs are for diagnostics only.
3. **Three roles.** Chat designs bounded TASK.md files and writes prose.
   Claude Code executes them against real line numbers. **Zeus runs
   every solve, every runner, every commit.** Chat and Code run nothing.
4. **Never trust a list Chat supplies.** Every "established facts" list
   given in these sessions has contained at least one error. Code
   verifies against source and reports failures rather than correcting
   them silently. This caught roughly a dozen real errors in Phase A.
5. **The applied artefact beats register prose.** Where a register entry
   and a result file disagree, the file wins.
6. **Nothing is retired, declared stale, or superseded until its
   contents have been read.**

## 4. What Phase A did

**Every live runner now uses the sliced builder.** Fourteen were ported.
Six shared functions in `src/optimize_experiments.py` take an explicit
**required** `builder` keyword with no default, so no caller changes
silently. Only five dead functions and one suspended caller still
reference the annual builder. **The annual builder survives as a
deliberate comparison instrument** in `22_`, `23_` and
`probe_2_6_structure.py`, which solve both legs by design.

**`src/stochastic.py` was wired** as
`scripts/30_run_stochastic_redispatch.py` — fixed-plan re-dispatch,
two-stage evaluation, producing VaR, CVaR and infeasibility under a
fixed plan. It is the only method in the codebase that can observe the
chosen plan failing; Sobol re-optimises every draw and cannot.

**The NDC cap construction was re-sourced end to end.** The power share
is now inventory-over-inventory — NID 1.A.1.a (16,234 kt, p.36) over
BTR1 Sector 1 Energy (193,346.72 kt, p.33), both 2018, both AR5 — giving
8.3963%. It replaced an unsourced 217.0 Mt denominator whose stated
basis appears nowhere in NDC 3.0. Caps tightened 3.1–8.7%.

**Four parameters were sourced to primaries**: DSCR to Yescombe (2014)
§12.3.1 p.323, with `dscr_central` 1.30 declared as a Nigeria premium
over the book's 1.25 process-plant minimum; storage capacity credit to
SPP/NREL/LBNL with the summer-only caveat declared; the social discount
rate to the World Bank's Ramsey mapping; the demand elasticity to
Liddle & Hasanov (2023) at ε = 0.80, moving `organic_central` 4.0% →
3.9%.

**Rate coherence was fixed.** The bankability test had been discounting
at a 13.24% blend, 70% composed of a naira-basis 18% rate in a real-USD
model — the rate the F1 treatment had removed from the objective.
It now prices at the objective's own commercial basis; $F_0$ fell 15.7%,
making EaaS **harder** to self-finance than prior results showed.

**Two findings were added**: F-32 (deterministically-optimal plans carry
zero adequacy margin) and the Frontier B / leakage work. **Both F-30 and
F-32 require re-measurement in Phase B.**

## 5. Do these two things BEFORE Phase B

### 5.1 Fix `extract_reliability_dual`'s averaging

`src/optimize_experiments.py`, indexed branch. It averages over every
year that returned a dual, **not binding years only**, despite its own
comment claiming otherwise. Non-binding `≤` constraints return 0 under
complementary slackness and drag the mean down. Pre-existing and
builder-invariant.

**Fix**: report the mean over binding years *and* the mean over all
years with a recorded dual, *and* the binding count — three fields
rather than one. Nothing is lost and the ambiguity becomes visible.
Affects `dual_usd_per_twh` and `dual_usd_per_pct_pt` in REL-2 and REL-3
only; `binding` is computed separately and is unaffected, and REL-1
hits the scalar branch.

**Why first**: REL-2 and REL-3 are items 15 and 16 on the re-run list.
Running them with a known-defective dual means running them twice.

### 5.2 Settle `09_`'s `reliability_mode="total"` empirically

`09_` uses the deprecated horizon-total alias while everything else uses
`"annual"`. §3.5.4 already records that the horizon-total form returned
an **identical plan across a 2.5-fold tightening** — it does not
discriminate between service standards. REL-1's ε-sweep may be equally
uninformative.

**Run REL-1 at both modes** — 112 solves instead of 56, about 40
seconds. It either confirms REL-1 is sound or reveals that its results
do not vary. Either outcome is reportable.

### §3.1.8 must be rewritten

It currently describes a two-builder split as a general scope condition
across nine runners. That is no longer true. **Every live runner uses
the sliced builder**, including `01b_`'s regression anchor — which Code
found was *already* sliced, so the section was inaccurate even before
the port. The section should now say: the two builders exist and differ
**structurally** rather than in resolution (no conversion, no hydro
dispatch, no storage energy-arbitrage value in the annual one — this
justifies §3.1.3's dominance proof and must stay); **every reported
result comes from the sliced builder**; and the annual builder's
remaining role is as a deliberate comparison instrument in `22_`, `23_`
and `probe_2_6_structure.py`.

Also correct, in §3.1.8 and §3.3.5: "cannot value storage" should read
**"cannot value storage's energy-arbitrage contribution, only its
firm-capacity credit."**

### What §3.6 must contain

The arm system and required-key discipline, with its three exceptions
declared (`social_discount_rate`, `within_block_peaking_factor`, and the
20 silent defaults). The two anchors and what re-baselining means. The
sweep at **39 invocations** (A 5 / B 26 / C 8) — Section C is
interaction cells, of which **four** are genuinely two-dimensional, not
all eight. ε-constraint on two objective pairs. Sobol with its epoch and
its prior-width caveat — `gas_availability_factor`'s U(0.295, 0.80)
spans the whole distance between observed NERC availability and the
nameplate assumption, so its 90% variance share is a property of the
prior, as the script's own note says. Fixed-plan re-dispatch, including
F-32's method-level finding that exact variable fixation is infeasible
against an LP's own optimum where a constraint binds at zero slack.

**The closing statement, on evidence**: deterministic, single-objective
at every solve, perfect-foresight; trade-offs recovered by ε-constraint;
uncertainty addressed at three separate levels. **No weighted-sum
objective** — it existed, was dead code, and was deleted.
